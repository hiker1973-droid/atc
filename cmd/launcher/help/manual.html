1. How Readback ATC works

Readback ATC runs on the PC that hosts your SRS server, next to DCS. Each controller is a separate program that joins SRS like a radio and listens on its own frequency.

  1. A pilot transmits on SRS. The controller on that frequency records the call.
  2. Speech to text: the recording goes to OpenAI's speech recognition, which returns the words.
  3. The controller decides what was asked - taxi, check in, picture, ball - using the mission's weather and runways and, where it needs positions, the live air picture from Tacview.
  4. Text to speech: the answer is spoken with the voice you chose (Windows, OpenAI or ElevenLabs), given a radio sound, and transmitted back on the same frequency through SRS.

Readback ATC never changes your mission or DCS itself. It is a voice on the radio: DCS still runs the AI tankers, carriers and airfields.

2. Before the first flight

  1. Finish Setup (it opens after install): squadron, keys, SRS server and password, theatre, then the test call. A test call you hear in SRS proves the whole voice path.
  2. On the dashboard pick your theatre and press Start. Each controller turns green as it joins SRS.
  3. In SRS, tune a tower frequency and call: "Batumi Tower, Viper 1-1, radio check." You should hear "Viper 1-1, Batumi Tower, loud and clear."
  4. Give your pilots the frequencies (section 17) and this manual's call tables, or the shorter tables in section 4 onward.
  5. Leave the dashboard running during your event. The Live Transmission Log shows every call as it happens.

4. Radio technique

Readback ATC listens like a real controller: it answers calls addressed to it, from a callsign it can hear.

  • Address first, then your callsign, then the request: "Batumi Tower, Viper 1-1, request taxi."
  • Use a callsign with a number - Viper 1-1, Venom 2-1-2 or a modex such as Raider 302 - and keep it the same all flight. A call without a number is not answered.
  • Key, pause half a second, then speak. Release and wait 2-3 seconds for the answer; don't key over it.
  • Keep it under 20 seconds. Two short calls beat one long one.
  • Numbers: say them naturally - "state 6.5", "angels 12", "seven DME", "runway 13".
  • Flights: the lead talks for the flight - "Batumi Tower, Viper 1-1, flight of two, ready for takeoff." One clearance covers everyone.
  • Didn't catch it? "Batumi Tower, Viper 1-1, say again." repeats the last answer to you.
  • Common mishearings are corrected for you (for example Reader or Rater becomes Raider), and a field name a letter or two off still counts.

The call tables below are the wordings Readback is tested against; similar wordings work too. The examples use Batumi and Viper 1-1 - use your field and callsign.

6. ATIS, weather and runways

  • Weather comes from the mission DCS is running: wind, ceiling, visibility, altimeter and temperature. When the server loads a different mission, every controller reloads its weather.
  • The runway in use is chosen from the wind, like a real tower. Every controller at a field agrees on it, and the ATIS says the same.
  • ATIS loops on its frequency. Its information letter moves on (Alpha, Bravo ...) only when the weather or runway changes, so hearing Alpha for hours is normal.
  • Set Weather on the dashboard sets the wind, ceiling, visibility and altimeter for the towers, Marshal and Deckboss straight away; Read from .miz fills in the mission's values again.
  • Carrier case: Marshal picks Case I or Case III from the ceiling, visibility and night. The dashboard's case selector can force Case I or Case III; Auto goes back to the weather.

In the groove Paddles calls what it sees, up to once a second while you are off: a little high, you're high, you're low, power, right for lineup, come left, you're fast / slow. No ball call by about 0.6 nm brings "call the ball". "Wave off, wave off, wave off" is mandatory: power and climb straight ahead. Paddles grades every pass (_OK_, OK, (OK), ---, cut, bolter, wave-off) with the wire in its log; DCS's own LSO grade for each trap goes to the greenie board on the Stats page.

A full carrier cycle

Deckboss (launch) → Marshal (on departure: clear seven miles) → Command for the mission → Command hands you back to Marshal at about 30 nm → Marshal (stack, Charlie, commencing, initial) → Paddles (break, abeam, ball, trap) → green deck → Deckboss.

11. The dashboard

Open Readback ATC from the Start menu, or http://localhost:7000 in a browser on the server.

Header

  • First row: your squadron, this server, the pages (Fleet, Logs, Pilots, Reports, Stats, Settings), the SRS and Tacview status, and the colour button.
  • Second row: the theatre switch, the region buttons that start and stop a theatre, and badges for your licence or trial days and the build.
  • Colours: the button in the header steps through Dark, Light, Night (red, for a dark room) and Auto (follows Windows). Settings > Appearance does the same. Each browser remembers its own choice.
  • Banners under the header say when the trial is running out, a licence needs renewing, an update is ready, or Setup is not finished.

Panels

12. Logs, Fleet and Pilots

  • Logs: each controller's full log, live, with filters. The first place to look when a call went unanswered: it shows the exact words heard.
  • Fleet: when you run Readback ATC on more than one server, each one's health, version and air picture on one page.
  • Pilots: if your server runs DCSServerBot, its pilot statistics (time in slot, kills, landings, traps) by day and by pilot.

15. Reports: After Action Reports

The Reports page makes an After Action Report of a session: a summary page with the result, a relief map of every kill and the pilot, engagement and missile tables; a page per engagement with the fight's tracks and missiles and every shot (range, launch altitude and speed, kill or miss); the pilots' sorties, flight time and carrier grades; the Safety items (blue-on-blue, ground impacts, ejections); and the AAR itself - BLUF, plan, sustain / improve and recommendations with owners; and, as you choose, a comms score for each pilot, a page per pilot, currency, weapon effectiveness, losses, the carrier recovery and the tankers. Every number and map is worked out on this PC; Claude writes the words, with your own Claude API key (Settings > Reports; create one at console.anthropic.com).

  1. Pick the session's records, each found on this PC or uploaded: the mission file (.miz: what was planned), the Tacview recording (.acmi or .zip.acmi: the tracks) and the DCS server's dcs.log (the shots, kills, takeoffs, landings and grades DCS scored). It must be the server's log (Saved Games\DCS.dcs_serverrelease\Logs on the server); a game client's dcs.log has none of them.
  2. Leave Include the radio logs ticked to add what was said on Readback's frequencies. The Comms page needs these logs. On a PC that watches several servers, choose which one flew the session, or leave whichever rig heard the session to read every server's logs and use the one with the most calls. If none had calls in that window, the preview says where it looked.
  3. Optionally say what to focus on, e.g. "Venom flight's carrier recovery".
  4. Under Report contents, tick the pages you want (the page remembers your choice). A page the session has nothing for is left out and listed at the end under "Not in this report".
  5. Press Preview: the report's pages appear with everything but Claude's words, and you can read exactly what will be sent.
  6. Press Write the AAR (or Save the report with Claude's write-up unticked: no key needed). Then Download PDF, Print, or post it: the Discord post boxes hold the debrief in your squadron's format, split into messages that fit Discord, each with Copy; attach the PDF or the picture of the summary page.

16. Stats: flight stats and the greenie board

The Stats page adds up every mission run in the DCS server's own logs on this PC (the live dcs.log, dcs.log.old and the dated .zip copies DCS keeps), plus any server logs you upload there: each pilot's sorties, flight hours, landings, traps, kills, losses, crashes, ejections and hit rate, and the greenie board of every carrier pass the DCS LSO graded. Pick this month, last month, the last 30 days, this year, all time or your own dates, then Export PDF, or the Pilots CSV and Greenie board CSV for a spreadsheet.

Greenie board points: _OK_ 5, OK 4, (OK) 3, bolter 2.5, --- 2, wave-off 1, cut 0. A wave-off for a foul deck is not the pilot's and is not scored. The page also shows your servers' ElevenLabs credits.

18. When something is wrong

What you seeLikely causeWhat to do
No answer at allController not running, wrong frequency or modulation, or the call didn't start with the station's name.Check its card is green; check the frequency and AM/FM; open Logs to see whether it heard you and what it heard.
It answered someone else, or the wrong thingThe words were misheard.Logs shows the words heard. Key, pause, then speak; station first, callsign with its number; a little slower. Check SRS isn't clipping your microphone.
"Say again your callsign"No callsign with a number was heard.Repeat with the full callsign.
Silence on every stationSRS password, OpenAI key, or voice output.Send the test call in Setup: it reports exactly what is missing. Check the OpenAI key's Test in Settings.
"I don't see you on scope", no picture, no bearingsTacview isn't reaching Readback.Turn on Tacview's real-time telemetry on the server and check its address in Settings > Server connection. The header shows Tacview's status.
AWACS off stationNo AWACS aircraft airborne in the mission, or it just restarted.Give it a minute; check the mission has its AWACS flying.
Wrong weather or runwayControllers are reading a different mission.Restart the theatre after loading the mission, or use Read from .miz in Set Weather.
A changed frequency or voice didn't takeThe role hasn't restarted.Settings > Roles > Restart this theatre's roles.
Voices sound plain (no radio effect)ffmpeg isn't installed.Run the installer again and let it install ffmpeg (see the Installation Guide).
A report has no shots or killsThe dcs.log is a client's, not the server's.Use the server's Saved Games\DCS.dcs_serverrelease\Logs\dcs.log.

See the Installation Guide for installing, the trial, licences and updates.

Report a problem: still stuck? Open Help → Report a problem (or the link under Updates in Settings). Say what happened, roughly when, and on which station, then press Make the report. Readback downloads one zip with your description, its versions, settings and the end of each log, with every key and password removed. Post the message it gives you in #bug-reports on our Discord and attach the zip.

TermMeaning
AngelsAltitude in thousands of feet: angels 12 = 12,000 ft.
BRAABearing, range, altitude, aspect of a contact from you.

Clara"I don't see the ball" (the carrier's landing light).

Joker / BingoFuel to start heading home soon / fuel to go home now.
Marking momsYour bearing and range from the carrier ("mother").
